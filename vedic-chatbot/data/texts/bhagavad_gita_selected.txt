[SOURCE: Bhagavad Gita] [CHAPTER: 1] [VERSE: 1]
Dhritarashtra said: O Sanjaya, what did my sons and the sons of Pandu do when they had assembled on the sacred field of Kurukshetra, eager for battle?

Sanskrit: धृतराष्ट्र उवाच | धर्मक्षेत्रे कुरुक्षेत्रे समवेता युयुत्सवः | मामकाः पाण्डवाश्चैव किमकुर्वत सञ्जय ||
Transliteration: Dhritarashtra uvaca | dharma-ksetre kuru-ksetre samaveta yuyutsavah | mamakah pandavas caiva kim akurvata sanjaya ||

Commentary: The battle of Kurukshetra is not merely a historical event but a metaphor for the internal war within every human being — the war between dharma (righteousness) and adharma (unrighteousness), between higher and lower impulses.

[SOURCE: Bhagavad Gita] [CHAPTER: 2] [VERSE: 47]
You have a right to perform your prescribed duties, but you are not entitled to the fruits of your actions. Never consider yourself the cause of the results of your activities, and never be attached to not doing your duty.

Sanskrit: कर्मण्येवाधिकारस्ते मा फलेषु कदाचन | मा कर्मफलहेतुर्भूर्मा ते सङ्गोऽस्त्वकर्मणि ||
Transliteration: karmany evadhikaras te ma phalesu kadacana | ma karma-phala-hetur bhur ma te sango 'stv akarmani ||

Commentary: This is the most celebrated verse of the Bhagavad Gita. Krishna teaches Arjuna the doctrine of Nishkama Karma — selfless action without attachment to results. One should perform one's duty diligently but surrender the fruits to the Divine. This teaching is the foundation of Karma Yoga.

[SOURCE: Bhagavad Gita] [CHAPTER: 2] [VERSE: 19-20]
He who thinks that this (soul) is a slayer and he who thinks that this (soul) is slain, both of them fail to perceive the truth: this neither slays, nor is it slain. It is never born, nor does it die at any time. It has not come into being, does not come into being, and will not come into being. It is unborn, eternal, ever-existing, and primeval. It is not slain when the body is slain.

Sanskrit: य एनं वेत्ति हन्तारं यश्चैनं मन्यते हतम् | उभौ तौ न विजानीतो नायं हन्ति न हन्यते || न जायते म्रियते वा कदाचि- न्नायं भूत्वा भविता वा न भूयः | अजो नित्यः शाश्वतोऽयं पुराणो न हन्यते हन्यमाने शरीरे ||

Commentary: Krishna explains the immortal nature of the Atman (soul). The soul cannot be killed, burned, wetted, or dried. It is eternal, indestructible, primeval. Death is merely the shedding of the physical body, not the end of the self.

[SOURCE: Bhagavad Gita] [CHAPTER: 2] [VERSE: 48]
Be steadfast in yoga, O Arjuna. Perform your duty and abandon all attachment to success or failure. Such evenness of mind is called yoga.

Sanskrit: योगस्थः कुरु कर्माणि सङ्गं त्यक्त्वा धनञ्जय | सिद्ध्यसिद्ध्योः समो भूत्वा समत्वं योग उच्यते ||
Transliteration: yoga-sthah kuru karmani sangam tyaktva dhananjaya | siddhy-asiddhyoh samo bhutva samatvam yoga ucyate ||

Commentary: Samatvam — equanimity — is the very definition of Yoga here. The practitioner neither celebrates success nor is crushed by failure. This mental steadiness (sthitaprajna) is the mark of a true yogi.

[SOURCE: Bhagavad Gita] [CHAPTER: 3] [VERSE: 3]
The Lord said: O sinless Arjuna, I have already explained that there are two classes of men who realize the Self. Some are inclined to understand it by empirical, philosophical speculation, and others are inclined to understand it by devotional work.

Sanskrit: श्रीभगवानुवाच | लोकेऽस्मिन् द्विविधा निष्ठा पुरा प्रोक्ता मयाऽनघ | ज्ञानयोगेन साङ्ख्यानां कर्मयोगेन योगिनाम् ||

Commentary: Krishna outlines two principal paths to liberation: Jnana Yoga (path of knowledge, favored by Sankhya philosophers) and Karma Yoga (path of action). Both lead to the same goal — moksha (liberation) — but suit different temperaments.

[SOURCE: Bhagavad Gita] [CHAPTER: 4] [VERSE: 7-8]
Whenever there is a decline in righteousness and an increase in unrighteousness, O Arjuna, at that time I manifest myself on earth. To protect the righteous, to annihilate the wicked, and to reestablish the principles of dharma, I appear on this earth, age after age.

Sanskrit: यदा यदा हि धर्मस्य ग्लानिर्भवति भारत | अभ्युत्थानमधर्मस्य तदात्मानं सृजाम्यहम् || परित्राणाय साधूनां विनाशाय च दुष्कृताम् | धर्मसंस्थापनार्थाय सम्भवामि युगे युगे ||
Transliteration: yada yada hi dharmasya glanir bhavati bharata | abhyutthanam adharmasya tadatmanam srjamy aham | paritranaya sadhunam vinasaya ca duskrtam | dharma-samsthapanarthaya sambhavami yuge yuge ||

Commentary: This is the famous promise of divine incarnation (Avatar). Krishna declares that whenever dharma declines, He takes birth to restore cosmic order. This is the theological basis of Vaishnavism's dasavatara (ten avatars) doctrine.

[SOURCE: Bhagavad Gita] [CHAPTER: 6] [VERSE: 5-6]
A man must elevate himself by his own mind, not degrade himself. The mind is the friend of the conditioned soul, and his enemy as well. For him who has conquered the mind, the mind is the best of friends; but for one who has failed to do so, his mind will remain the greatest enemy.

Sanskrit: उद्धरेदात्मनात्मानं नात्मानमवसादयेत् | आत्मैव ह्यात्मनो बन्धुरात्मैव रिपुरात्मनः ||
Transliteration: uddhared atmanatmanam natmanam avasadayet | atmaiva hy atmano bandhur atmaiva ripur atmanah ||

Commentary: This is one of the most psychologically profound verses. The mind can be our best ally or our worst enemy. Through yoga, meditation, and self-discipline, the mind can be tamed and made a friend that leads toward liberation.

[SOURCE: Bhagavad Gita] [CHAPTER: 9] [VERSE: 22]
But those who always worship Me with exclusive devotion, meditating on My transcendental form — to them I carry what they lack, and I preserve what they have.

Sanskrit: अनन्याश्चिन्तयन्तो मां ये जनाः पर्युपासते | तेषां नित्याभियुक्तानां योगक्षेमं वहाम्यहम् ||

Commentary: This verse expresses the ultimate assurance of bhakti (devotion). God promises to personally take responsibility for the welfare (yoga = attainment, kshema = preservation) of those who surrender completely to Him. It is the theological basis of prapatti (surrender) in Vaishnavism.

[SOURCE: Bhagavad Gita] [CHAPTER: 18] [VERSE: 66]
Abandon all varieties of dharma and simply surrender unto Me alone. I shall liberate you from all sinful reactions; do not fear.

Sanskrit: सर्वधर्मान्परित्यज्य मामेकं शरणं व्रज | अहं त्वां सर्वपापेभ्यो मोक्षयिष्यामि मा शुचः ||
Transliteration: sarva-dharman parityajya mam ekam saranam vraja | aham tvam sarva-papebhyo moksayisyami ma sucah ||

Commentary: This is the charama shloka — the final and most important verse of the Gita according to many Vaishnava traditions. It is the ultimate teaching: surrender completely to the Divine, transcend all limited definitions of dharma, and receive the grace of liberation.
